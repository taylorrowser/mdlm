# Product specification

A PSP is a concise map of product intent, not a requirements dump.

1. State the problem independently of a proposed solution.
2. Name concrete users or stakeholder groups.
3. Separate goals, measurable success, scope, constraints, and non-goals.
4. Describe only essential workflows at user-intent level.
5. Prefer links to detailed lifecycle data over duplicated commitments.
6. Expose uncertainty as QST and consequential assumptions as DEC.
7. Remove implementation design, speculative features, and unsupported metrics.

A strong PSP lets a reviewer explain why the product should exist and what success
means without dictating how the system will be built.
